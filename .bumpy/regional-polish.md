**See what a character means in Chinese.** The Regional forms view now lists the first meanings English Wiktionary gives a character in Chinese, so you can see where a meaning has moved: 湯 is "broth; soup" in Chinese, and "hot or boiling water" is marked literary. **From Wiktionary** now gives each language's etymology under its own heading: how the forms relate, where each Japanese word written with the kanji came from by its reading (国's くに from Old Japanese, こく from Middle Chinese), and where the Chinese shape came from. The notes show their lists as lists, and fold the rest under **More from Wiktionary**. Select a form in the chart to copy it. Readings start with Japan's, and Cantonese tone numbers are raised and explained. On a kanji's page, **Regional forms** is now a plain link after **Stroke order** and **Component tree**.
