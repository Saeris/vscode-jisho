---
title: Particles used with verbs
chapter: Basic Grammar
---

The first particle to learn is the object particle, because it is a very
straightforward particle. The を character attaches to the end of a word to
signify that the word is the direct object of the verb.

#### Examples

1. {魚|さかな}を{食べる|た・べる}。
   Eat fish.

1. {ジュース|ジュース}を{飲|の}んだ。
   Drank juice.

Unlike the direct object familiar from English, places can also be the direct
object of motion verbs such as {歩|ある}く and {走|はし}る.

1. {街|まち}をぶらぶら{歩く|ある・く}。
   Aimlessly walk through town.

1. {高速|こう・そく}{道路|どう・ろ}を{走る|はし・る}。
   Run along the expressway.

When you use する with a noun, the を particle is optional and the whole
[noun + する] can be treated as one verb.

1. {毎日|まい・にち}、{日本語|に・ほん・ご}を{勉強|べん・きょう}する。
   Study Japanese every day.

## The target に particle

The に particle marks a target. Unlike を, which marks what the verb acts on,
に marks where it is directed.

1. {図書館|と・しょ・かん}に{行きました|い・きました}。
   Went to the library.

1. {友達|とも・だち}に{電話|でん・わ}を{掛けた|か・けた}。
   Called a friend.

Note the difference between に and で: に marks the destination of a motion,
while で marks where an action takes place.

1. {家|いえ}で{勉強|べん・きょう}します。
   Study at home.
